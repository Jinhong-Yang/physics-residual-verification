\textbf{Research Article submission}\par
Dear Editors of IEEE Access,

Please consider our manuscript, ``Protocol-Constrained Residual Adaptation with Frozen Vision--Language Features for Rigid-Body Inference,'' by Hyojin Park, Nam-Hyun Yoo, and Jinhong Yang, for publication as a \textbf{Research Article}.

The study examines whether a compact visual residual can add predictive information after a strong numerical estimator has already used a short motion observation. The method combines fixed robust estimation, numerical residual adaptation, and a bounded frozen-feature residual with protocol-specific permissions. Its contribution is the tested allocation of roles and the measured increment, rather than a claim that hybrid estimation or VLM-based physical inference is new in isolation.

On 72 new synthetic geometry families, the complete method reduces canonical analytic new-action error by 29.95\% relative to the strongest fixed robust point baseline. Most of the improvement comes from numerical adaptation; the visual increment is 1.07\%, with one-sided support and a two-sided interval containing zero. A separate offline public simulator task yields a 4.27\% visual-plus-gate increment after task-specific development fitting. The manuscript distinguishes this result from zero-shot transfer and reports conservative empirical coverage and the unresolved real-video transfer result.

We believe that the combination of structured estimation, controlled visual-feature use, and inspectable evaluation is relevant to the applied artificial intelligence and engineering readership of IEEE Access. The accompanying materials include manuscript source, figures, supplementary implementation notes, and a portable replay of saved paired statistics. The public-task selection and group-independence limitations are stated explicitly. AI assistance and research funding are disclosed in the manuscript.

The supplement also reports cached-feature component experiments, a mean-fixed covariance sensitivity analysis, and initialization-Jacobian diagnostics. These analyses retain the original model and are explicitly distinguished from independent confirmation.

A further new-episode study separates both object and surface friction conditions during refitting. On 329 evaluation episodes, it retains a 4.38\% material-pair improvement with a crossed-material 95\% interval of $[-6.044,-0.116]$ mm. The manuscript distinguishes this evidence from new object/scene-asset transfer and discloses historical architecture selection within the material bank. Versioned verification materials are provided through the linked public GitHub repository.
